# What graph conditioning changes in the generated text

Interpretive reading of `comparisons.md` (57 judge comparisons, gpt-5.4-mini, gold abstract as reference; Generation A = Vicuna baseline, Generation B = adapter). No counts or scores here by design — qualitative patterns only, grounded in the judge's own wording.

**A caveat on the evidence before anything else.** The judge outputs are heavily templated — almost every entry opens "Generation B is closer to the gold abstract than Generation A because…" and pivots on "However, B still misses…". That structure suggests the judge prompt framed the question as *whether and why B is closer*, which biases the write-ups toward finding a reason B wins. The bias is not total — the judge does push back in a visible minority of cases ("Generation B is **not closer** to the gold abstract than Generation A") — but the report below should be read as an analysis of *what the judge saw*, filtered through that framing.

## 1. The mechanism of improvement

When the adapter wins, the closeness is almost always the same kind: **it locates the paper in the right region of topic space.** The judge's recurring formula is "it at least stays in the right topic area / the right broad problem family," and what fills that slot is consistently three things at once:

- **The right research problem.** "It correctly centers the paper on the right comparison: 'the use of non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) in multiuser MIMO systems' and explicitly contrasts it with 'multi-user beamforming'" (40380); "correctly centers the problem as model-free RL for an MDP with a temporal-logic specification" (40475).
- **The right methodology family.** "'Markov Decision Process (MDP),' 'model-free reinforcement learning (RL),' and 'Linear Temporal Logic (LTL)'" (40475); "'inverse optimal control' / 'inverse reinforcement learning,' 'recovering an unknown reward function'" (50421).
- **The right domain vocabulary.** "'human part-aligned representation,' 'regions (parts),' and 'body part misalignment problem'" (44943); "'skin lesion analysis,' 'dermoscopic images,' and 'ISIC 2017'" (45692).

In the text these are not separate phenomena. They co-occur in nearly every win and the judge treats them as one move: the adapter's abstract is *about the right thing*, and problem statement, method family, and vocabulary all follow from that. Occasionally the closeness extends one level deeper, to the **motivating gap** — the best wins get this: "it explicitly says classification networks produce 'small and coarse discriminative object regions' and then describes a refinement process" (136982), or the deployment-constraint framing "computational cost… is still high" (92508). But gap-framing closeness is the rarer, stronger form of the same phenomenon, not a different one.

The one-sentence claim I would defend: **the baseline's characteristic failure is writing a fluent abstract of the wrong paper, and the adapter's contribution is topic anchoring — it reliably fixes *which paper family* is being written about, not what that paper specifically did.**

## 2. Topic drift: confirmed for the baseline, refined for the adapter

The suspicion — that the baseline latches onto one neighbour and writes *that* paper's abstract — is well supported. The judge repeatedly describes A not as vague but as **specifically about a different, recognizable paper**: "A drifts into a different paper about 'a low-feedback NOMA scheme' and 'TDD and FDD modes'" (40380); "A's unrelated 'Invertible cGANs (IcGANs)'" (41782); "A is mostly about 'frustum pointnets,' 'RGB-D data'" (43171); "Generation A's unrelated DenseNet compression paper" (101728); "A's very specific but off-target focus on 'Bezier developable surfaces, discrete geodesic nets, and NURBS curves'" (134934); "ParseNet-style semantic segmentation … which is a different paper entirely" (99924). These are named methods and datasets from the literature — exactly what abstracts of individual neighbours would contain. The baseline's failure is copying, with high specificity, a single wrong source.

The refinement the text forces is this: **the adapter does the same thing, at a shorter topical distance.** B also substitutes identifiable other papers — "it substitutes a different paper entirely—'large margin cosine loss (LMCL)… CosFace'" (53098); "the model name is wrong ('MobileFaceNet' instead of 'SeesawFaceNets')" (126782); "it replaces Scene Representation Networks with an unrelated 'Deep3D' GAN" (43254); "invents a different method, 'Dynamic Generative Memory (DGM)'" (97779); "B instead describes Stacked GANs" (103971). So the honest formulation is not "the baseline drifts and the adapter stays on topic." It is: **both systems generate the abstract of *some* plausible paper rather than *this* paper; the adapter's imagined paper lives in the right neighbourhood, the baseline's often does not.** Graph conditioning shrinks the drift radius; it does not eliminate the drift.

## 3. The ceiling

What the adapter systematically fails to recover is remarkably uniform, and the judge names it the same way almost every time: "B still misses the core contribution." Concretely, three layers never survive:

- **The paper's named method and its mechanism**: "no 'Distribution Distillation Loss,' no teacher/student distributions" (53098); "no mention of mesh representation, graph convolutional network, iterative deformation of a coarse mesh" (87158); "nothing about blossoms, the rational functions Λ, M, ν" (134934).
- **The numbers and the conclusions**: "the reported result improvement from '67% to 79%' on '1.6M question answering pairs'" (51622); "the gold's main conclusion that 'multi-user beamforming gives the highest average sum rate'" (40380); "the exact efficiency gains like '66%' computational cost or '54.2%' parameters" (126782).
- **The experimental scaffolding**: dataset lists, evaluation claims, code-release statements.

Does the line fall on "knows the neighbourhood → gets the topic" vs. "would need the paper → can never get the result"? **Largely yes, and the pattern is almost too clean:** a paper's contribution is precisely its delta over its neighbours, so it is by construction absent from the conditioning signal, while its topic is exactly what the neighbourhood is dense in. The failures sit right on that line.

But two things complicate the tidy version. First, the adapter does not fail gracefully at the line — it **confabulates across it**. Rather than producing a vague, hedged abstract where the specifics should be, it fills the gap with invented specifics drawn from the neighbourhood distribution: "invents unsupported details such as 'MobileFaceBlocks,' 'MobileConvBlock,' 'MobileMaxPoolBlock,' and evaluations on 'COCO' and 'ImageNet'" (126782); "invents task examples like 'cartpole swing-up, dexterous manipulation, legged locomotion'" (140925). The ceiling is not silence about the contribution; it is a fluent, wrong contribution.

Second, there is a failure mode that is not about missing specifics at all: **genre**. Survey papers fail as a class. The neighbourhood of a survey is full of method papers, so the adapter writes a method paper: for a CNN-architecture survey, "Generation B instead invents a specific proposed model, 'Residual-Attention Network (RAN)'" (98612); for an IoT-communications survey, "B frames the work as a single 'novel deep learning-based method for radio signal classification,' while the gold is a 'comprehensive survey'" (126535); similarly the BCI cybersecurity survey (134751) and the smart-buildings survey (105246). A paper's *kind* — survey, position paper, framework paper (134891's "unifying conceptualization" is missed the same way) — is not the modal kind of its citations, and the adapter inherits the mode. There is also a purely textual degeneration mode that has nothing to do with graph conditioning: several B outputs are "highly repetitive and truncated" (134751), "repeating 'We use a convolutional neural network to detect text lines in images' many times" (48949), with "obvious repetition" also flagged at 140009 and 141459.

## 4. The non-improvements

The cases the judge flags as not-better ("Generation B is not closer…") are isolated relative to the wins but have a discernible common character: **papers whose identity cannot be read off the topical centre of their neighbourhood.** They come in a few flavours:

- **The contribution sits at a different level of abstraction than the neighbours.** The Dual Focal Loss paper (149478) is a *loss-function* paper embedded among segmentation *application* papers, so B writes a segmentation-architecture abstract ("contracting path," "symmetric expanding path") and "never mentions loss functions, class imbalance … or auto-tuning." The A3 paper (117731) is a *hardware accelerator* paper among attention-mechanism papers, and B writes about "a hierarchical attention mechanism," missing "hardware accelerators for attention" entirely.
- **Fine distinctions inside a dense sub-field.** Weight Standardization (136978) drowns among other normalization papers — B produces "a different normalization paper." The VQA-certainty paper (104423) gets swapped for its sibling task: B "talks about 'generate natural language questions from an image'… unrelated to the gold's method and task." Tellingly, the exemplar VQA/VQG paper (138816) suffers the mirror-image confusion — B "misstates the task as question generation." When the neighbourhood contains near-identical siblings, centroid-level conditioning cannot resolve which sibling this is.
- **Idiosyncratic papers.** The reservoir-computing RL paper (95207) uses "a 'fixed random-weight CNN,' 'reservoir computing,' and 'evolution strategy'" — an unusual method its deep-RL neighbourhood gives no hint of. The Quizbowl paper (105215) is a quirky trivia-competition dataset paper; B substitutes the generic "NewsQA."
- **Surveys**, as above (105246 is both a non-improvement and a survey).

Notably, in a couple of these the *baseline* lands closer partly by accident: for the Bayesian-DP federated learning paper (107063), "A … at least mentions 'differentially private federated learning' and 'Bayesian differential privacy for machine learning,' which are closer to the gold's actual subject" — in a vocabulary-dense sub-field, copying one neighbour can beat aggregating the neighbourhood, because one neighbour may be the right one.

The actively-worse cases deserve the sharpest statement: at 104423 and 105215 the judge says B "is actually farther away in topic and framing," and at 105246 "actually much farther off," with B collapsing into "a generic, repetitive overview of 'The Internet of Things (IoT)'." The pattern in all three is **over-generalization to the neighbourhood**: the adapter regresses toward the broad topical mean (generic IoT, generic QA-dataset, sibling task) while the baseline's single-neighbour copy happened to be, or contain, something closer. Graph conditioning buys nothing — and can cost something — exactly when the paper is an outlier from its own citation neighbourhood. These non-improvements occur across neighbourhood sizes (8 neighbours at 111914, 83 at 105215), so the text gives no support to "too few neighbours" as the explanation.

## 5. Worked examples

**Clean win — 40380 (NOMA vs. beamforming, 9 neighbours).** The baseline "drifts into a different paper about 'a low-feedback NOMA scheme' and 'TDD and FDD modes'" — a specific, plausible, wrong paper. The adapter instead "correctly centers the paper on the right comparison: 'the use of non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) in multiuser MIMO systems' … explicitly contrasts it with 'multi-user beamforming'" and even recovers the practical-analysis angle ("CSI is acquired through pilot signaling," "shared pilots"). What it cannot recover is the finding: that "multi-user beamforming gives the highest average sum rate" and that NOMA is "essential" only inside a "hybrid" scheme. This is the mechanism in miniature: question right, framing right, answer unrecoverable.

**Strong win at the ceiling — 161266 (RL-based question generation, 95 neighbours).** The judge's most positive verdict: B "correctly says the paper is about 'automatic question generation (QG)' from 'a given passage and answer,'" captures "a combination of supervised and reinforcement learning" with "policy gradient techniques," and names SQuAD — task, learning paradigm, and benchmark all correct. Yet even here it misses "the 'graph-to-sequence (Graph2Seq) architecture,' the 'bidirectional graph neural network (GNN) based encoder'" — the architectural contribution. Even the best case stops exactly at the topic/contribution line.

**Instructive ambiguous case — 53098 (face-recognition loss, 55 neighbours).** The adapter is "much closer" — fluent in the sub-field's vocabulary ("softmax loss," "center loss," "large margin softmax loss," "angular softmax loss") and right about the shared goal of "minimizing intra-class variance and maximizing inter-class variance." But "it substitutes a different paper entirely—'large margin cosine loss (LMCL)… CosFace.'" This is the clearest window into what the adapter actually does: it has learned the neighbourhood so well that it reproduces a *real neighbouring paper* rather than the target. The win and the ceiling are the same behaviour.

**Clean failure — 98612 (CNN-architecture survey, 96 neighbours).** The gold "classifies the recent innovations in CNN architectures into seven different categories." The adapter talks fluently about "residual learning," an "attention mechanism," and "the vanishing gradient problem" — the right ingredients — but "invents a specific proposed model, 'Residual-Attention Network (RAN),' and focuses on 'CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100' results." A survey's neighbours are method papers, so the adapter writes a method paper. Genre is invisible to neighbourhood conditioning.

## 6. The claim, and what this evidence cannot establish

**The claim:** Conditioning a frozen LLM on a GNN encoding of the paper's citation neighbourhood changes *what the generated abstract is about*. Without it, the model typically reproduces the abstract of a single wrong source — often recognizably one neighbouring paper. With it, the generation anchors on the target paper's actual research problem, methodology family, and domain vocabulary, and in the best cases its motivating gap; what it still cannot produce is the paper's contribution — the named method, mechanism, results, and conclusions — whose place it fills with fluent, neighbourhood-plausible confabulation, and it regresses to the neighbourhood's topical mean, which turns from feature to bug precisely for papers atypical of their own citations (surveys, cross-level contributions, near-duplicate sub-fields, idiosyncratic methods). In short: graph conditioning converts "a fluent abstract of the wrong paper" into "a fluent abstract of a plausible-but-nonexistent paper on the right topic."

**What this evidence cannot establish.** First, mechanism attribution: nothing here separates the graph *structure* (2-hop, GNN aggregation) from mere access to *more neighbour text* — a baseline given all k neighbours' abstracts well-aggregated might show the same topical anchoring. Second, the judge's framing appears to presuppose comparing "why B is closer," so the prevalence of B-wins in these write-ups is soft evidence at best; the quantitative side must carry that claim. Third, this is a single judge's reading of a 57-node sample with no human verification of the judge's quotes against the raw generations, and the sample was stratified by neighbour count, not by paper type — so the survey/outlier failure classes are observed but their true prevalence is unknown. Finally, "closer to gold" is closeness of description, not usefulness: whether a topically-correct confabulated abstract is *more* dangerous than an obviously off-topic one (because it is harder to spot as wrong) is a real question this comparison cannot answer.
